Washer suspension design and dynamic balancing

A front-load washer tub has to stay in its operating position while the drum spins. I designed suspension components, specified them with GD&T, and sized the counterweights that set the balance.

Problem and constraints

Two connected objectives. The suspension had to control vibration and system dynamics while keeping the tub within its required operating position. Separately, the sub-washer had to be held at its required operating tilt, which is set by where mass sits relative to the center of rotation.

Approach: suspension

  • Designed suspension components using machine-elements principles and factor-of-safety analysis.
  • Developed drawings and schematics with GD&T for manufacturing and assembly, with schemes to ASME Y14.5-2009 and technical specifications for three subsystems.
  • Evaluated spring placement and load paths to optimize the tub-to-spring interaction and system behavior.

Approach: dynamic balancing

  • Calculated counterweight mass, density, and placement.
  • Determined weight locations that achieve the required center of gravity and tilt angle.
  • Calibrated the counterweights around the rotating assembly.

Verification

Suspension performance improvements were validated against legacy designs through testing, to support design decisions. The factor of safety above 2 is a theoretical result from the design-phase analysis, not a test measurement.

Results

  • Completed the design phase with a theoretical factor of safety above 2.
  • Established suspension geometry and component designs that meet the required operating conditions.
  • Achieved consistent centroid placement relative to the center of rotation across all models.
