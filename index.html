3D-Printed Centrifugal Compressor Personal project · Oct 2024 – Jan 2025

A personal project to design and test a 3D-printed centrifugal compressor. The impeller was designed parametrically, simulated in ANSYS, and redesigned based on the results.

Parametric modeling in SOLIDWORKS

  • Fit the blades within the housing, targeting a 50 mm diameter and 10,000 RPM, with blade geometry dependent on the other blades.
  • Defined blade shape with equation-based splines, varying spline degree and complexity by formula and technique.

ANSYS simulation feedback

  • Calculated volumetric flow rate and visualized vortices and fluid stagnation within the volute.
  • Used results to manipulate design variables and resolve design flaws in the parameterizable model.

Reflection and results

  • Refining the blade equation improved geometric control with minor performance impact.
  • Design constraints identified: zero-thickness geometry, unmanufacturable conditions, and insufficient design constraints.
  • Validated performance with an anemometer. Measured airflow was within ±20% of the predicted 24.55 CFM.
  • Key issues: excessive shaft friction, PLA deformation, dimensional variation, and unintended surface friction from 3D printing.
  • Next steps: functional tolerancing, material selection for the application, and better drafting techniques (DFM).
